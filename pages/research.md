    <h3 class="entry-title">"Preferential" Trade Agreements?</h3>
    <p class="entry-coauthors">with Jan Stuckatz</p>

      <div class="abstract">
        <p>
          We show that preferential trade agreements (PTAs) often fail to generate effective tariff preferences. Although PTAs offer tariffs below most-favored-nation (MFN) rates, gradual phase-outs combined with unilateral MFN reductions can eliminate these advantages. When unilateral MFN reductions undercut preferential tariffs, the negotiated advantage disappears until implementation catches up. We find that half of the 356 PTAs notified to the WTO do not adjust preferential rates after MFN cuts, making such reversals potentially common. Using Colombian transaction-level data on the U.S. PTA, we show that 35% of imports expected to benefit did not effectively receive preferences in early years. A triple-difference analysis indicates that affected imports were 34% lower than comparable imports from third countries. These findings challenge standard views of PTAs and carry important policy implications.
        </p>
      </div>

    <h3 class="entry-title">Green trade squeeze: the rules-rewards gap in EU green trade policy</h3>
    <p class="entry-coauthors">with Caroline Bertram</p>

    <div class="entry-links">
      <details class="abstract-toggle">
        <summary>Abstract</summary>
      </details>

      <span class="divider">·</span>
      <details class="bibtex-toggle">
        <summary>BibTeX</summary>
      </details>

      <div class="abstract">
        <p>
          The EU’s green trade agenda is facing growing backlash. The EU carbon border adjustment mechanism (CBAM) and deforestation regulation (EUDR) have, in particular, become highly contested both within the EU and among trade partners, feeding broader debates about a roll-back of the European Green Deal. This article explores a simple idea: the backlash may partly reflect a widening rules-rewards gap in EU green trade policy, where environment-related market-entry requirements have increased (rules), without parallel effort to deliver increased market-access through preferential trade agreements (rewards). This study investigates this claim by analysing market-access provisions across all recent EU preferential trade agreements (PTAs) concluded between 2010-2025, focusing on the product categories covered by CBAM, EUDR and environmental goods and services. For these product areas, we assess whether preferential treatment has increased over time, especially since 2019. We find little evidence of systematic market opening. Recent EU PTAs appear not to offset stricter environmental market-access requirements with corresponding gains in preferential treatment, suggesting a rules-rewards gap. Amid green backlash, this imbalance may have weakened political support and reduced the durability of the EU’s green trade turn. More broadly, the findings invite reflection on whether sustaining trade-environment governance may require a credible balance between environmental market-access conditions and shared economic opportunities in the green economy.
        </p>
      </div>

  <article class="entry">
    <h3 class="entry-title">Country-Level Explanations for the Underutilization of Preferential Trade Agreements</h3>
    <p class="entry-coauthors entry-placeholder">&nbsp;</p>

    <div class="entry-links">
      <details class="abstract-toggle">
        <summary>Abstract</summary>
      </details>

      <span class="divider">·</span>
      <details class="bibtex-toggle">
        <summary>BibTeX</summary>
      </details>

      <div class="abstract">
        <p>
          What explains utilization of preferential trade agreements? Contrary to common perception, preferential tariffs negotiated within trade agreements are not automatically applied. In fact, firms must decide whether to utilize preferential tariffs for each transaction. Existing explanations for underutilization of preferential tariffs highlight too little benefits or too high administrative costs. This paper uses the case of European Union PTAs, which offer identical benefits and demand identical administrative requirements across its member states. It presents two main findings. First, the paper shows striking cross-country differences in preference utilization across EU member states. Thereby, it offers novel, descriptive evidence that existing product-level explanations do not suffice to explain underutilization of PTAs. Second, the paper explores the role of national institutions in the implementation of PTAs, using panel data on utilization rates for EU imports between 2002 and 2022. Contrary to theoretical expectations, the paper finds a negative relationship between EU country’s quality of institutions and PTA utilization. The paper concludes that, for understanding preference utilization, more micro-level evidence is needed regarding the actual process of applying for preferential tariffs, firms’ perception of that process, and the administrative processes of national customs authorities.
        </p>
      </div>
