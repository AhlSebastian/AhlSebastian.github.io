title: '"Preferential" Trade Agreements?'

abstract: |
  We show that preferential trade agreements (PTAs) often fail to generate effective tariff preferences. Although PTAs offer tariffs below most-favored-nation (MFN) rates, gradual phase-outs combined with unilateral MFN reductions can eliminate these advantages. When unilateral MFN reductions undercut preferential tariffs, the negotiated advantage disappears until implementation catches up. We find that half of the 356 PTAs notified to the WTO do not adjust preferential rates after MFN cuts, making such reversals potentially common. Using Colombian transaction-level data on the U.S. PTA, we show that 35% of imports expected to benefit did not effectively receive preferences in early years. A triple-difference analysis indicates that affected imports were 34% lower than comparable imports from third countries. These findings challenge standard views of PTAs and carry important policy implications.
